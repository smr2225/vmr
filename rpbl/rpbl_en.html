Mars. 2225. The Second Republic.

This is a site about a possible future — not fiction, but a visionary reconstruction. About the Second Martian Republic: a society that could not have emerged on Earth. Survival here is far more demanding than at the coldest pole of Antarctica — because it is colder, the atmosphere is nearly absent, there is no closed cycle of water, oxygen, or organic matter, and yet the regolith contains toxic, aggressively corrosive perchlorates, surface gravity is low (38 % of Earth’s), and solar and cosmic radiation levels are high. Earth is so distant that its influence is practically imperceptible.

Under these conditions, any life-support failure is fatal. Any uncontrolled zone poses a threat to all.

Societies modelled on Earth — democratic, hierarchical, bureaucratic, based on freedom or coercion — did not survive the first crises. Hence: Mars. 2225. The Second Republic.

But where did the people on Mars come from, if few even choose to live in Antarctica? They were not those who wanted Mars — but those who wanted to leave Earth. For various reasons: some fled justice, others pursued abstract science or progress, while others sought escape from unbearable existence or were swayed by the campaigns of philanthropists and patrons. Yet they had to devise new forms of coexistence entirely on their own.

Mars was colonised because life is expansion: without it, life stagnates and perishes. And Mars is the most viable candidate for building a new world. The Moon is too close to Earth to allow sufficient autonomy.

Here, we examine why this became possible.

Councils

On Mars, decisions are made quickly and meaningfully — where the problem arises. Councils give every citizen not only the right to speak, but also the opportunity to turn their voice into action. Here, a voice matters not as a mark in statistics, but as part of a common search for a solution. This is how power is formed that responds to the rhythm of the environment itself. A citizen expresses an opinion in the primary council, where a decision is discussed and made; if necessary, it is transmitted upward through a delegate. The higher council considers the problem and returns the decision to the citizens. Here, the citizen's voice always belongs to the citizen and is transmitted by their delegate, under their control.

The ethical system makes this process even more refined and pleasant: you can share your assessment of any phenomenon — from the rise of Phobos to the taste of lunch or a new station project. Each assessment is a signal for a neighbor, for councils, for the entire society. This is how living feedback works, keeping us in a single rhythm.

Culture

For us, labor is culture. Culture is mainly reproduced and improved by education: it transmits knowledge and skills, verifies capability, opens the path to professions. But for us, culture is also generated and constantly developed in the practice of life itself, in research, art, language, in our shared existence. It manifests in images, customs, sciences, arts, in how we design spaces, how we communicate and think. The Supreme Council approves its standards, local councils care for their observance, development, and diversity.

Earthlings divide culture into elite and mass, viewing it as a resource for enrichment and entertainment. For us, culture is what makes us a society, its breath and meaning.

Labor

As soon as conscious, purposeful labor distinguished humans from the animal kingdom and allowed creating more product than required for life, conditions were created for socialist distribution of labor products for the benefit and development of society and individuals. But historically, for millennia, another principle prevailed — exploitation of man by man. We derived the formula for socialist expanded production and established a system of labor, evaluation, and distribution according to this formula, which allows naturally conducting socio-economic activity based on socialist principles and enjoying the main advantages of socialist production: freedom of labor choice, absence of exploitation, total planning.

Economy

The SMR economy is a planned, highly developed, automated and robotized system of reproduction and development. We work for society four hours a day, and this is enough to provide everything necessary and more. Everything necessary is distributed through public mechanisms, and the additional — through APS (Additional Public Property). The share in APS depends on the quality of labor: training, talent, effort, intensity, necessity. Each labor is invested in the common good, and the contribution returns. Luxury, excess, is produced in a special capitalist circuit — yes, it exists, but does not control society, only serves ambitions. Thus, the economy becomes a tool for sustainability and expansion of society, mastering the Solar System, not a goal to earn money.

The Second Martian Republic is practice. We left to survive. We stayed to live. We live to develop. We are waiting for you.
